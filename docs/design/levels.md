# Design — the A1 to B2 path

Our own syllabus for the Spanish track, organised on the CEFR scale
(Common European Framework of Reference). Content is data: the ladder in
`content/es/track.json` is the source of truth, and this document is the plan
behind it.

Levels are a property of the **track**, not of the learner. One learner walks
A1 → A2 → B1 → B2; the app does not need an account to know where someone is,
it reads their item states.

## Where the grammar progression comes from

The **order** of the grammar points follows the sequence in a published
beginner course outline — articles, then *ser*, then the regular present
tense, then *estar*, then the irregulars, and on through the preterite. A
syllabus of grammar points is a functional outline, and it is a well-tested
one.

Everything else here is ours: every word, translation, note, example and
exercise. No lesson text, exercise or glossary is taken from any published
book. Where a book is worth reading, it is on the reading list, not in the
content.

**Exit criteria** — a learner has finished A1 when they can order, ask for
directions, introduce themselves and describe their routine, in short exchanges.

**The first fifteen units teach the grammar; the next thirty-five build the
vocabulary.** The first fifteen are the ones that carry a new rule each, so
they are the spine of the level. After that the course widens: home, food,
the town, work, health, time, and the words for describing things, all built
on the rules the first fifteen taught. That is how a real A1 course is
weighted: a small set of grammar units, and a large set of thematically
organised vocabulary.

## Rules for writing a unit

1. **Ten words.** The unit size comes from the reference app: a unit is a
   short, finishable session (`0 / 10` on the progress bar).
2. **A unit teaches one thing.** One grammar point, one theme. A unit that
   mixes three grammar points teaches none of them.
3. **The words are the content.** The exercises are generated from them by
   `scripts/build-content.mjs`; nobody writes an exercise by hand.
4. **Every word carries a note.** A short grammar or usage note, in our words.
   The feedback panel shows it after the answer.
5. **A wrong answer is a word the learner has already met.** The generator
   enforces it, and a test checks it: a wrong answer the learner has never
   seen is a new word wearing a disguise.
6. **Four shapes, rotating.** `select`, `match`, `type_in`, `listen` — a
   learner should not meet the same shape three times in a row.

## The path has no gaps

Three rules, all enforced by code rather than by hope:

- **A unit opens when the one before it is finished.** The first unit of the
  path is always open; every later one needs its predecessor complete. The
  server refuses to start a locked unit, so the path cannot be jumped.
- **"Finished" means every exercise has been seen.** Not a score, not a
  streak — every exercise shown. A skipped exercise counts as seen, because a
  skip is free and must not hold the path open. A learner who struggled still
  moves on.
- **Finished is not the same as learned.** A unit the learner got wrong
  (below eight right answers in ten) is *weak*, and weak units are offered
  back on the dashboard under *Worth another look*. A finished unit can always
  be started again.

The locks are recomputed from the item states on every request, so the next
unit opens the moment the last exercise of the one before it is answered.

## And that is checked, not hoped for

`npm run audit:path` walks the whole path in order and fails on three kinds of
hole. It runs as part of `npm test`, so a gap fails the build.

1. **Structural** — every level runs 1..N with no missing numbers.
2. **Conceptual** — no unit uses a grammar point before the unit that teaches
   it. A lesson that points forward ("you will meet that in B1/6") is allowed;
   a lesson that quietly assumes it is not, is not.
3. **Complete** — every unit has a lesson, some examples, some drills, and ten
   words.

The audit is what caught the preterite sitting ten units too late: A2 taught
the imperfect and leaned on the preterite to explain it, while the preterite
itself was only taught in B1.

## Content gap

132 units × 10 words = **1320 words** for the Spanish track. At ten words a
unit, that is one hundred and thirty-two small vocabulary files rather than
one hundred and thirty-two large exercise files — the generator does the rest.

A0 holds 20 words, A1 holds 500, A2 holds 320, B1 holds 260 and B2 holds 220.
Every level now has the size of a real course at its stage: A1 is complete
at 500, and the higher levels widen the world instead of reteaching the
grammar.

`listen` needs audio. The schema, the renderer and the browser's own voice are
in place; recorded audio would replace the voice and change nothing else.
